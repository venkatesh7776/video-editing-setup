# VibeLevel Screening — Outcome-Led POV
## Video Script + Visual Direction for Claude Code

**Purpose:** A polished, cinematic product demo for founders, CEOs, HR leaders, and recruiters.
**Format:** 16:9 landscape.
**Creative direction:** Outcome-led POV. Use real product UI to prove capabilities and restrained motion design to explain ideas. Keep the visuals premium, clean, legible, and purposeful.

## Before editing: inspect the website and assets

Before creating the video:
1. Explore the existing VibeLevel website. Identify the real brand colours, fonts, logo treatment, UI patterns, and visual tone.
2. Capture the clearest available assets for the landing page, assessment creation, templates, candidate workspace, and report.
3. Use the supplied image named **`Claude Code screen`** for Scene 5.
4. Use the report link supplied by the user for Scene 7. Capture genuine report sections; do not fabricate report UI, scores, or candidate information.
5. For Scenes 6–7, decide after reviewing the real assets whether product UI, motion graphics, or a restrained combination communicates each idea best.
6. If a required screenshot or link is missing or inaccessible, flag it and request the asset. Use a neutral placeholder in the plan rather than inventing product functionality.

## Global visual rules

- Use the real brand system discovered on the website. Do not guess colours or fonts.
- When showing the VibeLevel logo on a brand-colour background, keep the background clean: **no decorative lines or patterns behind the logo**.
- Use large, readable typography and short on-screen phrases. The voiceover carries the full explanation.
- UI zooms must land on the exact section being discussed and hold long enough to read.
- Use controlled push-ins, pull-outs, and pans. Avoid constant motion and random zooms.
- Use motion graphics to clarify the evaluation approach, not to imply unverified features.
- Keep spelling exact: **VibeLevel Screening**, **Core Skills**, **Working with AI**, **Ownership**, **Human Contribution**.
- Blur private candidate details. Do not invent scores, metrics, product features, or performance claims.
- Prefer genuine product screenshots/screen recordings when a scene is intended to demonstrate the product.

# Scene-by-scene visual direction

## Scene 1 — Hook

**Voiceover**

> “When AI can write the code, build the solution, and do the work, what are you really hiring for?”

**Visual concept:** A memorable recruiter character looks toward the viewer. Place the character on the **right side** of the frame, with oversized kinetic typography on the **left**.

**Animation**
1. Start with a clean, minimal frame and the recruiter character on the right.
2. On **“When AI can”**, bring the words onto the left side. Start visually distant or oversized, then animate into a controlled zoom-out/settle.
3. On **“write the code”**, bring in a new, prominent text beat with a purposeful zoom-in.
4. Repeat the typographic rhythm for **“build the solution”** and **“do the work”** without overcrowding the frame.
5. On **“what are you really hiring for?”**, clear or recede the previous phrases and reveal the question as the strongest text beat. Keep the recruiter visible as the visual anchor.

**On-screen text beats**
- “When AI can”
- “write the code.”
- “build the solution.”
- “do the work.”
- “What are you really hiring for?”

**Notes:** Use strong contrast, keep text clear of the character, and make each zoom feel intentional rather than like a slideshow.

## Scene 2 — The Challenge

**Voiceover**

> “Writing prompts is one thing. Knowing what to build, how to guide AI, and whether the result actually works requires real technical skills, judgment, and ownership.”

**Visual concept:** Abstract motion design showing the difference between simply writing a prompt and doing the deeper work of planning, guiding, and validating a solution.

**Animation**
1. Begin with a simple prompt card or typed text.
2. On **“Writing prompts is one thing”**, keep the prompt card simple and quickly resolve it.
3. On **“Knowing what to build”**, assemble structured blocks into a solution, suggesting planning and problem framing.
4. On **“how to guide AI”**, animate a sequence such as input → AI-assisted output → human adjustment.
5. On **“whether the result actually works”**, show a result being checked against a requirement. Do not use fake product UI or random code as decoration.
6. On **“technical skills, judgment, and ownership”**, let three clear visual elements resolve into a stable composition, emphasizing each phrase in turn.

**Possible on-screen labels:** “Prompting”, “Problem-solving”, “Guiding AI”, “Validating the result”, “Skills · Judgment · Ownership”.

**Notes:** Motion should communicate prompt → decisions → verification. Do not imply a specific AI-detection method.

## Scene 3 — Introducing VibeLevel Screening

**Voiceover**

> “That’s where VibeLevel Screening comes in. Through realistic, role-specific tasks, it evaluates candidates across four dimensions: Core Skills, Working with AI, Ownership, and Human Contribution.”

**Visual concept:** This is the main brand reveal. When **“VibeLevel Screening”** is spoken, show the VibeLevel logo on a solid brand-green or approved brand-colour background. Then transition to the real landing page, using centre-focused zooms into relevant areas.

**Animation**
1. On **“That’s where VibeLevel Screening comes in”**, show the logo on a solid brand-colour background. No decorative lines, patterns, or busy effects.
2. Hold briefly, then transition to the genuine website/landing page.
3. On **“realistic, role-specific tasks”**, push in toward a relevant product section or genuine product visual.
4. On **“four dimensions”**, transition to a clean **2×2 quadrant grid**.
5. Reveal one quadrant exactly when each pillar is spoken:
   - **Core Skills:** quadrant 1; use a visual suggesting role-related skill or task performance.
   - **Working with AI:** quadrant 2; show a person guiding and checking AI output.
   - **Ownership:** quadrant 3; suggest decisions, follow-through, or responsibility.
   - **Human Contribution:** quadrant 4; suggest human judgment, context, or original contribution.
6. Highlight the active quadrant subtly while keeping the others quieter. End with all four visible and balanced.

**Notes:** Use exact pillar names. The grid should be clear and uncluttered. Illustrations are explanatory graphics, not claims that those exact icons are product features.

## Scene 4 — Assessment Creation & ATS Integration

**Voiceover**

> “Start by creating an assessment using a ready-made template, customized to your role requirements. Or use AI to build one by simply describing what you need. You can also integrate with Lever or Ashby to connect assessments to your existing hiring workflow.”

**Visual concept:** Use actual dashboard screenshots or screen recordings. Show assessment creation briefly; it is supporting context, not the main USP.

**Animation**
1. Start with the real assessment dashboard/creation screen.
2. On **“ready-made template”**, zoom into template selection and hold long enough to read.
3. On **“role requirements”**, pan or zoom into the relevant configuration fields.
4. On **“use AI to build one”**, show the genuine AI-assisted creation interface and zoom into its prompt area. If unavailable, request a screenshot rather than inventing the UI.
5. On **“Lever or Ashby”**, show the genuine integration UI if available. Otherwise use approved logos or simple text labels without implying a specific connection status.
6. Pull back to the full UI and transition to the candidate workspace.

**Notes:** Use high-resolution captures and slow, readable zooms. Use approved third-party brand assets.

## Scene 5 — The Candidate Experience

**Voiceover**

> “Candidates work on realistic, role-specific tasks in a dedicated workspace with Claude Code built in. As they work with AI to solve problems, VibeLevel looks beyond the final result to understand how they approach the task.”

**Visual concept:** The user-provided image named **`Claude Code screen`** is the primary reference. Make this feel like a real candidate working, not a generic code animation.

**Animation**
1. Begin with the screenshot and gently push in to establish the workspace.
2. On **“realistic, role-specific tasks”**, focus on the task brief/requirements area, if visible.
3. On **“dedicated workspace with Claude Code built in”**, move to the workspace and Claude Code areas.
4. On **“work with AI to solve problems”**, pan subtly between the task and AI interaction. Do not fabricate messages or code.
5. On **“looks beyond the final result”**, pull back to reveal the wider workspace, then transition into evaluation visuals.

**Notes:** Blur private information. Avoid excessive cursor/typing effects. The audience does not need to read every line of code.

## Scene 6 — Core Skills & Working with AI

**Voiceover**

> “Core Skills reveals how candidates perform against role-specific competencies. Working with AI evaluates how they guide AI, make decisions, and verify its output.”

**Visual concept:** Inspect the website and report assets first. Prefer real UI if it clearly demonstrates role-specific skills or AI collaboration. If the UI cannot explain the idea, use clean motion graphics and transition into the real report.

**Animation**
1. On **“Core Skills”**, reveal the real competency/skill-score section if available. Zoom into its labels or breakdown; never invent scores.
2. If no suitable UI exists, use a simple graphic with skill dimensions resolving into a competency card. Make it clearly illustrative, not a real candidate report.
3. On **“Working with AI”**, show a human-guided sequence: instruction → AI suggestion → human review/adjustment.
4. On **“make decisions”**, emphasize the human decision point.
5. On **“verify its output”**, show the result being checked against requirements.
6. End with both concepts in a connected composition, ready to transition to Scene 7.

**Possible on-screen labels:** “Role-specific competencies”, “Guide”, “Decide”, “Verify”.

**Notes:** Do not use random code snippets, invented metrics, or claims of certain AI detection. The focus is how candidates work, not merely whether AI was used.

## Scene 7 — Ownership, Human Contribution & Report

**Voiceover**

> “Ownership and Human Contribution reveal the responsibility candidates take and the value they bring beyond AI-generated output. The assessment report brings together skill scores, AI collaboration insights, Code Review, and Video Q&A. The Interview Guide turns strengths and gaps into focused follow-up questions.”

**Visual concept:** For Ownership and Human Contribution, follow Scene 6’s approach: real UI where useful, otherwise restrained explanatory motion graphics. For the report, use genuine screenshots captured from the report link provided by the user.

**Animation**
1. On **“Ownership”**, show a genuine product/report element if one exists. Otherwise, use a simple human-led action moving from decision to completion.
2. On **“Human Contribution”**, emphasize context, judgment, or value beyond a generated answer. Avoid generic stock imagery.
3. On **“The assessment report brings together”**, transition into the real report screenshot.
4. On **“skill scores”**, zoom into the relevant score/competency summary.
5. On **“AI collaboration insights”**, pan to the relevant report section.
6. On **“Code Review”**, zoom into the real Code Review section if present. Do not imply it applies to every assessment type unless confirmed.
7. On **“Video Q&A”**, move to that genuine report section.
8. On **“The Interview Guide”**, zoom into the real guide and hold on the follow-up questions.
9. Pull back to show the report as a cohesive evaluation view, then transition to Scene 8.

**Notes:** The supplied report link is the source of truth. Do not fabricate sections, scores, quotes, or insights. If the link is inaccessible, request screenshots or an accessible link. Do not add an appendix explanation or repeat the four pillars here.

## Scene 8 — The Outcome

**Voiceover**

> “See beyond a candidate’s final output. Understand their strengths, identify gaps, and build a shortlist backed by evidence.”

**Visual concept:** Use a high-quality background image or cinematic abstract background that supports confident hiring. Overlay oversized typography timed to the voiceover.

**Animation**
1. Start with a calm background image, with subtle motion or a gentle pan.
2. On **“See beyond a candidate’s final output”**, bring in the first headline with a confident scale-up/focus-in.
3. On **“Understand their strengths”**, transition to the next phrase and use subtle background movement.
4. On **“identify gaps”**, emphasize the words without an alarmist treatment.
5. On **“build a shortlist backed by evidence”**, reveal the strongest final phrase and let the composition settle.
6. Transition cleanly into the end card.

**Possible on-screen text**
- “See beyond the final output.”
- “Understand strengths.”
- “Identify gaps.”
- “Build a shortlist backed by evidence.”

**Notes:** Avoid generic handshake stock imagery. Keep text contrast high and copy short.

## Scene 9 — End CTA

**Voiceover**

> “When AI can do the work, know what makes a candidate stand out.  
> VibeLevel Screening.  
> Book a demo.”

**Visual concept:** A clean, premium branded end card using the VibeLevel logo and approved brand colour.

**Animation**
1. Transition to a solid brand-colour background or approved branded end-card layout.
2. Show **“When AI can do the work, know what makes a candidate stand out.”**
3. Reveal the VibeLevel logo and **“VibeLevel Screening”**.
4. Bring in **“Book a demo”** as the final CTA.
5. Hold long enough to read, then end cleanly.

**Notes:** No decorative lines behind the logo. Do not add a URL unless the user provides or approves it.

## Final quality checks
- [ ] Voiceover matches the supplied script exactly unless a change is approved
- [ ] Each UI zoom corresponds to the spoken line
- [ ] All product UI comes from the website or supplied assets
- [ ] No fabricated product features, scores, metrics, or performance claims
- [ ] No private candidate information is exposed
- [ ] Text is legible at normal playback size
- [ ] Transitions and easing feel consistent
- [ ] Logo reveals use a clean background without decorative lines
- [ ] The film prioritizes evaluation approach and evidence over candidate invitation/sharing mechanics

## Recommended production order
1. Inspect the website and assets.
2. Make an asset map: one visual source per scene.
3. Confirm missing screenshots/report access before building those scenes.
4. Build a voiceover timing pass and rough animatic.
5. Animate Scenes 1–3 to establish the visual language.
6. Add genuine product UI for Scenes 4–7.
7. Create the outcome and CTA cards.
8. Review legibility, pacing, factual accuracy, and total duration before final render.
